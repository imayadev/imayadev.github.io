Tooth Fillings - Are They Actually Good For Your Teeth?

By Laiba Choudhry · Age 12 (Junior Category) · Dame Alice Owen's School · 13th March 2026

Tooth fillings are a common dental restoration used to repair a cavity in your tooth, usually caused by decay or to fix broken/worn out teeth. If untreated, it can lead to severe toothaches, or infections which lead to having your tooth extracted.

The procedure that occurs during a tooth filling includes: a dentist usually numbing the tooth area and surrounding gum tissue with a local anesthetic to ensure that the patient doesn’t feel any pain during the procedure, then comes the removing of the decay where a high-speed drill removes the decayed portion of the tooth to make sure the area is cleaned properly.

The next step is the preparation for the filling where if you are getting a filling with composite resin, then, an acid gel (etch) is applied to roughen the enamel for better adhesion, followed by a bonding agent that is hardened with a special curing light.

After this step, it’s now time for the filling placement where composite or amalgam, (different types of fillings) can be used. Composite is added in thin layers with each layer cured and Amalgam is packed into the cavity and is shaped, where it will harden on its own.

The final step is the shaping and polishing where the dentist will shape the filling to make it resemble the natural features of your tooth, ensuring a comfortable and functional bite. After this procedure your mouth may remain numb for a few hours and you might be sensitive to hot or cold things. These are all completely normal things that you might experience.

The different types of fillings your dentist can give you, include, composite resin which is tooth-coloured, it blends in with your natural teeth, and it's most often used for visible areas in your teeth. Amalgam which is a durable, silver-coloured mixture of chemicals that is often used for your back molars. Gold/Ceramic is long-lasting, durable and typically more of the expensive options. Fillings are very durable, if applied or tooth extractions.

The primary purpose of a filling is to remove the decayed portion of the tooth and seal the cavity. This action prevents bacteria from spreading deeper into the tooth, which, if left untreated, could lead to more serious issues like nerve damage, infection, a root canal, or even tooth loss.

Decay or damage can make chewing and biting painful or difficult. A filling restores the tooth's original shape, strength, and function, allowing for comfortable eating and drinking. Exposed areas from decay often cause sensitivity to hot, cold, or sweet foods and beverages. The filling covers and protects the sensitive inner parts of the tooth, eliminating this discomfort. By intervening early with a filling, the dentist can remove only the necessary decayed material and preserve more of the natural tooth structure compared to more extensive treatments like crowns.

If you get rid of decay with a filling early, then the dentist will only have to remove the necessary decayed enamel and can preserve more of the natural tooth structure compared to more extensive treatments like crowns. Also it can save you from needing more complex and expensive treatments in the future.

Modern, tooth-coloured fillings, such as composite resin, can be matched to your natural tooth colour. This makes them almost nearly invisible and an excellent choice for visible areas, making the appearance of your smile look perfect. Furthermore, many composite fillings don’t have mercury in them so it is a much safer alternative to amalgam fillings which contain mercury.

However, there can be several downsides to a tooth filling, for example. Bypost-procedure sensitivity, which is the likelihood of needing future replacement, potential for cracks or fractures in the restored tooth, and aesthetic or material-specific drawbacks like staining (composite) or mercury content (amalgam).

They can also cause discomfort if the decay was deep. Your teeth may remain sensitive to pressure, cold, or air for several weeks following the procedure. Deep fillings can cause persistent aching. Fillings are not permanent and they can wear out, crack, or break over time, requiring replacement.

The process may require removing more natural tooth structure, and large fillings can leave the tooth to be more likely to get fractures. Amalgam fillings are highly visible, not tooth-colored. They can cause the surrounding tooth to turn grey or black and may cause cracks due to expansion and contraction in your mouth. Also concerns about mercury exposure exist, though they are generally considered safe.

Composite fillings are mainly more expensive to place than amalgam. They may stain over time from coffee or red wine and can wear down faster under heavy biting pressure. Decay can sometimes develop underneath an existing filling.

I experienced a disadvantage of having a tooth filling. I went to the dentist to treat a cavity, and the dentist removed the decay and placed a filling in my tooth. However, shortly after the procedure I began to feel a sharp pain that gradually became worse. When I returned to the dentist, they told me that the tooth had become infected and that I would need an extraction to prevent the infection from spreading. I eventually had the tooth removed to avoid further complications. This experience shows a disadvantage of fillings, because if all of the decay is not completely removed before the filling is placed, bacteria can remain under the filling and the cavity can continue to develop, which may lead to infection and tooth loss.

In conclusion, I think that overall tooth fillings are good for your teeth because fillings can help your teeth to restore your damaged enamel and it will protect your teeth from having greater damage. However, it can also have disadvantages, such as, the filling breaking or cracking over time or the filling causing persistent aching in your tooth if you had a deep filling.
